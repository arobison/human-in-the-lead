# 20. Ideas this design uses

[Back to the index](../0001-human-in-the-lead.md)

Each entry gives an idea, its source and where this design uses it. Systems theory comes first: the design is a set of nested feedback loops whose goals and bounds the owner sets.

Links were checked on 2026-11-14; sources without one are cited from their standard bibliographic details and were not reread.

## 20.1 Cybernetics and systems theory

- **Requisite variety.** A regulator can control only as much variety as it has. The system matches routine variety with rules and precedent and sends the owner only what is novel. W. Ross Ashby, _An Introduction to Cybernetics_ (1956), <https://archive.org/details/introductiontocy00ashb>. [Part 1](../0001-human-in-the-lead.md#11-what-human-in-the-lead-means), [part 4](04-decision-rights.md#42-when-the-owner-is-involved), [part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model).
- **The good regulator.** Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system. Roger C. Conant and W. Ross Ashby, "Every Good Regulator of a System Must Be a Model of That System", _International Journal of Systems Science_ 1(2), 1970. The data model keeps a field only if a person or a controller acts on it ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Near-decomposability.** Complex systems that last are built from parts whose internal links are dense and whose links to each other are few. Herbert A. Simon, "The Architecture of Complexity", _Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society_ 106(6), 1962. The work tree is for accountability, the `needs` graph for dependency, and milestones cut across both ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **The viable system model.** A viable system has five functions (operations, coordination, control, intelligence, policy), plus audit and alarm channels. Stafford Beer, _Brain of the Firm_ (1972) and _The Heart of Enterprise_ (1979). The completeness check in [part 19](19-viable-system-model.md); milestones as intelligence and releases as the boundary ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Stocks, flows and delays.** Behavior comes from stocks, the flows that change them and delays in feedback; acting on delayed signals makes a loop oscillate. Donella H. Meadows, _Thinking in Systems_ (2008). Queues report stock and flows; controllers act on leading indicators ([part 2](02-loops.md#common-rules)); the data model stores states and transitions and derives the rest, and a release is the flow out of the merged-but-unreleased stock ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Leverage points.** Changing a parameter moves a system least; changing its rules or goals moves it most. Donella H. Meadows, "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" (1999). Controllers move parameters; rules and goals are the owner's ([part 11](11-metrics-and-control.md#112-who-moves-what), [part 2](02-loops.md#loops-nested-by-speed)); milestones state goals as outcomes ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Double-loop learning.** Single-loop learning fixes an error; double-loop learning changes the rule that let it happen. Chris Argyris and Donald A. Schön, _Organizational Learning_ (1978). The learning loop turns defects and incidents into tested rule changes ([part 2](02-loops.md#28-learning)).

## 20.2 Control theory

- **Feedback and cascaded loops.** A controller compares a sensor with a setpoint and moves an actuator; in a cascade, a slow outer loop sets the setpoint of a fast inner one. Hysteresis and minimum samples stop loops chasing noise. Karl J. Åström and Richard M. Murray, _Feedback Systems_ (2008). Loops nested by speed, and stability rules for every controller ([part 2](02-loops.md#loops-nested-by-speed), [part 11](11-metrics-and-control.md#stability-rules)). A milestone is a setpoint, and its unmet clauses are the error ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Additive increase, multiplicative decrease.** Probe for capacity gently; back off sharply on congestion. Van Jacobson, "Congestion Avoidance and Control" (SIGCOMM 1988), DOI 10.1145/52324.52356. The concurrency controller, with merge conflicts and resource waits as congestion ([part 11](11-metrics-and-control.md#114-feedback-controllers)).

## 20.3 Queueing and flow

- **Little's law.** In a stable queue, average work in progress equals throughput times average time in the system. John D. C. Little, "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW", _Operations Research_ 9(3), 1961, <https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.3.383>. It sizes WIP limits and locates the constraint ([part 2](02-loops.md#common-rules)).
- **Cost of delay, WIP limits, small batches.** Cost of delay puts value and time in one unit; cost of delay over duration orders independent jobs best; small WIP and batches cut waiting. Donald G. Reinertsen, _The Principles of Product Development Flow_ (2009). The objective ([part 1](../0001-human-in-the-lead.md#13-the-objective)), the ordering rule ([part 2](02-loops.md#24-prioritization)), merge batches that shrink when failures rise ([part 2](02-loops.md#26-verification)), and thin milestones and frequent releases ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Value of information.** Measure only where the expected value of what you learn exceeds its cost. Douglas W. Hubbard, _How to Measure Anything_ (2007). Spikes and studies carry that value in the ordering rule ([part 2](02-loops.md#24-prioritization)), and learning items carry it as their contribution to a milestone ([part 3](03-data-model.md#milestone)).

## 20.4 Management

- **Theory of constraints.** Identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, elevate it, repeat. Eliyahu M. Goldratt and Jeff Cox, _The Goal_ (1984); the five focusing steps, <https://www.tocinstitute.org/five-focusing-steps.html>. Usually the owner's attention or the merge queue ([part 2](02-loops.md#common-rules)).
- **Appetite.** Set what a problem is worth first, then shape the work to fit. Ryan Singer, _Shape Up_ (2019), <https://basecamp.com/shapeup>. Appetite in tokens; the bet is the owner's ([part 2](02-loops.md#23-shaping)); a milestone's scope fills its appetite ([part 3](03-data-model.md#milestone)).
- **Mission command.** Give the intent and the end state, not the method, so the worker can adapt. Stephen Bungay, _The Art of Action_ (2010). An item says why and when it is done; the executor chooses how and logs its cheap-to-undo calls ([part 5](05-execution.md#the-executor-contract)).
- **One-way and two-way doors.** Take care over decisions that are hard to undo; make easy-to-undo ones fast. Jeff Bezos, letter to Amazon shareholders for 2015 (2016). One-way doors are the owner's; two-way doors default to action ([part 4](04-decision-rights.md#42-when-the-owner-is-involved)). A release is a one-way door for the public interface; cutting a milestone's contribution is a two-way door ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Must-be and attractive quality.** Some qualities only cause dissatisfaction when missing; others add satisfaction in proportion, or delight. Noriaki Kano et al., "Attractive Quality and Must-Be Quality", _Journal of the Japanese Society for Quality Control_ 14(2), 147–156, 1984. Must-be qualities are a milestone's constraints; the rest are contributions scored by value per token, in place of priority labels such as MoSCoW (Dai Clegg, 1994, later part of DSDM) ([part 3](03-data-model.md#milestone)).
- **Outcomes over output.** Measure the change in what users do, not the features delivered. Joshua Seiden, _Outcomes Over Output_ (2019). Milestones state outcomes for users ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).

## 20.5 Measurement

- **Goodhart's law.** A measure used as a target stops measuring. Marilyn Strathern's wording of Charles Goodhart's 1975 observation: "'Improving ratings': audit in the British University system", _European Review_ 5(3), 1997, <https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:eurrev:v:5:y:1997:i:03:p:305-321_00>. Donald T. Campbell said the same of social indicators ("Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change", 1979). Every target metric has a pair that worsens under gaming ([part 11](11-metrics-and-control.md#113-goodhart-pairs)); a milestone is done only when its independent check passes, never because its items are done ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Common and special causes.** A stable process's own noise needs no response; treating it as a signal makes things worse. Walter A. Shewhart, _Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product_ (1931); W. Edwards Deming, _Out of the Crisis_ (1986). Budgets ignore changes within noise ([part 2](02-loops.md#217-non-functional-budgets)), the eval gate fails only beyond measured variance ([part 17](17-evals.md#175-how-it-runs)), and a failing test is rerun before blame ([part 14](14-integration.md#147-flaky-tests)).

## 20.6 Decision science and human factors

- **Calibration.** Stated confidence should match how often one is right, and comparing recorded estimates with outcomes improves it (Hubbard, 20.3). Estimates are tracked for accuracy and precision ([part 1](../0001-human-in-the-lead.md#14-accuracy-and-precision)) and checked against outcomes ([part 2](02-loops.md#210-outcome-sensing)); owner judgments are recorded before the outcome, as in a decision journal ([part 3](03-data-model.md#judgment)).
- **The Brier score.** The mean squared gap between stated probabilities and what happened. It is a proper scoring rule: a forecaster gets the best expected score by stating what they believe. Glenn W. Brier, "Verification of Forecasts Expressed in Terms of Probability", _Monthly Weather Review_ 78(1), 1950. The calibration review scores calls with it ([part 23](23-people-in-the-lead.md#234-feedback-to-the-person-the-calibration-review)).
- **Probe, sense, respond.** Where cause and effect are clear only in hindsight, act by safe probes and let the results decide. David J. Snowden and Mary E. Boone, "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making", _Harvard Business Review_, November 2007. A learning milestone is done when its question has a verdict ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Calibrated trust.** Trust in automation should follow its record. John D. Lee and Katrina A. See, "Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance", _Human Factors_ 46(1), 2004, DOI 10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392. Trust calibration proposes autonomy per area and kind of decision; the owner decides ([part 2](02-loops.md#215-trust-calibration), [part 4](04-decision-rights.md#46-autonomy-changes-with-the-record)).

## 20.7 Machine learning

- **Proxy objectives and reward hacking.** An optimizer pushed on a proxy raises the proxy, not the goal; models have stubbed tests and read graders' answers to pass. Sydney Von Arx, Lawrence Chan and Beth Barnes, "Recent Frontier Models Are Reward Hacking" (METR, 2025), <https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/>. Improving agents cannot reach what grades them ([part 18](18-self-improvement.md#183-guardrails-against-gaming-its-own-grading)).
- **Held-out evaluation.** A score means something only on data the optimizer has not seen, and repeated queries leak a held-out set. Cynthia Dwork et al., "The reusable holdout: Preserving validity in adaptive data analysis", _Science_ 349(6248), 2015. The sealed eval set and its run budget ([part 18](18-self-improvement.md#2-a-sealed-eval-set-the-improving-agents-never-see)).
- **Evaluating agents on real tasks.** Grade agents on real issues by tests, and report pass^k, the chance of passing all k tries. Carlos E. Jimenez et al., "SWE-bench" (ICLR 2024), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770>; Shunyu Yao et al., "τ-bench" (2024), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045>. Replay of a project's own past items ([part 11](11-metrics-and-control.md#replay)); the eval suite's metrics ([part 17](17-evals.md#174-metrics)).

## 20.8 Software verification and delivery

- **Tests without an exact oracle.** Property-based tests check invariants over generated inputs, metamorphic tests relations between outputs, differential tests one implementation against another. Koen Claessen and John Hughes, "QuickCheck" (ICFP 2000); T. Y. Chen et al., "Metamorphic Testing: A Review of Challenges and Opportunities", _ACM Computing Surveys_ 51(1), 2018, DOI 10.1145/3143561; William M. McKeeman, "Differential Testing for Software", _Digital Technical Journal_ 10(1), 1998. Written from the spec, not by the implementer ([part 6](06-verification.md#63-test-kinds-and-the-adversarial-loop)).
- **Mutation testing on changed code.** Broken copies of the code show whether the tests notice; limiting mutants to changed lines makes it affordable. Goran Petrović and Marko Ivanković, "State of Mutation Testing at Google" (ICSE-SEIP 2018), <https://research.google/pubs/state-of-mutation-testing-at-google/>. [Part 6](06-verification.md#63-test-kinds-and-the-adversarial-loop).
- **Reliability growth.** Cumulative failures against cumulative exposure show whether reliability is improving; in the Crow-AMSAA model a growth parameter below 1 means it is. Larry H. Crow, "Reliability Analysis for Complex, Repairable Systems", in F. Proschan and R. J. Serfling (eds.), _Reliability and Biometry_ (SIAM, 1974), 379–410; summary at <https://www.reliawiki.com/index.php/RGA_Chapter_5>. Per area, to set human-read sampling rates ([part 6](06-verification.md#65-reliability-over-time)).
- **Merge queues.** Test each change against those queued ahead of it to keep the main branch green. Ananthanarayanan et al., "Keeping Master Green at Scale" (EuroSys 2019), <https://doi.org/10.1145/3302424.3303970>. [Part 5](05-execution.md#53-the-merge-queue), [part 14](14-integration.md).
- **Semantic versioning.** A version number states what changed in the public interface: breaking, additive or a fix. Tom Preston-Werner, Semantic Versioning 2.0.0 (2013). The interface is defined in [part 16](16-packaging.md#165-releases-and-compatibility); `hitld release propose` derives the bump from the interface delta alone ([part 3.0](03-data-model.md#30-principles-of-the-model)).
- **Continuous delivery.** Keep the mainline always releasable, so a release is a business choice rather than a technical event. Jez Humble and David Farley, _Continuous Delivery_ (2010). Releases can be cut at any merge, with no release train by default ([part 2](02-loops.md#27-shipping)).
- **Delivery measures.** Five measures of delivery throughput and stability. DORA, <https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/>. Adapted to merges ([part 11](11-metrics-and-control.md#111-measures)).

## 20.9 Security

- **Least privilege.** Run every component with the fewest privileges its job needs. Jerome H. Saltzer and Michael D. Schroeder, "The Protection of Information in Computer Systems", _Proceedings of the IEEE_ 63(9), 1975. Per role, enforced by agent definitions and hooks ([part 10](10-security.md#102-least-privilege-per-role)).
- **Prompt injection.** Text an agent reads can carry instructions; mitigations include least privilege, segregated content and adversarial testing. An agent with private data, untrusted input and an outbound channel can be made to leak. OWASP GenAI Security Project, "LLM01:2025 Prompt Injection", <https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/>; Simon Willison, "The lethal trifecta for AI agents" (2025), <https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/>. Quarantine, no role with all three, standing red-team evals ([part 10](10-security.md#101-threat-model)).
- **Trusting trust.** A self-compiling compiler can carry a flaw its source does not show; an independent rebuild exposes it. Ken Thompson, "Reflections on Trusting Trust", _Communications of the ACM_ 27(8), 1984, DOI 10.1145/358198.358210; David A. Wheeler, "Countering Trusting Trust through Diverse Double-Compiling" (ACSAC 2005), <https://dwheeler.com/trusting-trust/>. A self-change is judged by a different model or framing ([part 18](18-self-improvement.md#4-diverse-checking)).
- **Provenance.** Verifiable records of how an artifact was built. SLSA, <https://slsa.dev/>; in-toto, <https://in-toto.io/>. Commit trailers now, signed release attestations later ([part 6](06-verification.md#64-provenance)).
